# Absorbing discrete diffusion language model

## Historical question and bottleneck

[Structured Denoising Diffusion Models in Discrete State-Spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03006) (Austin et al., 2021) is the primary reference. The original question motivating this module is how to implement absorbing-mask corruption and reverse token prediction on text8 or TinyShakespeare. The practical bottleneck for this exercise is explicit: Absorbing transition rows sum to one; distinguish mask and padding. This is a reduced teaching adaptation, not a claim that the paper used our dataset or compute budget.

## Mechanism and equations

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x_t\mid x_{t-1})=\operatorname{Cat}(x_{t-1}Q_t),\\ q(x_t\mid x_0)=\operatorname{Cat}(x_0\bar Q_t),\quad\bar Q_t=Q_1\cdots Q_t,\\ (Q_t)_{mm}=1
\end{aligned}
$$

Here x is a row one-hot token, Q a row-stochastic transition matrix, Cat a categorical distribution, and m an absorbing mask state. Once masked the forward process stays masked; a learned reverse process reconstructs tokens.

Derive each tensor's dimensions before implementing the equation. State the normalization axis, numerical stabilizer and train/evaluation behavior. Distinguish a mathematical mechanism from the full original training system.

## Implementation notes

Input/task: **tinyshakespeare**. Output: **Corrupted tokens time -> clean-token logits**. Optimize or report **token_nll** in the **minimize** direction. Absorbing transition rows sum to one; distinguish mask and padding.

This is a draft skeleton: reference training and task-specific tests are not implemented. The explicit stub must not be mistaken for a finished model.

## Controlled experiment

Compare uniform and absorbing transition kernels at equal denoiser calls. Keep split, seed, optimizer schedule and training budget fixed unless that factor is the experimental variable. Report raw quality, wall time and parameter count, not an unsupported superiority claim.

## Pitfalls and checks

- Absorbing transition rows sum to one; distinguish mask and padding.
- Never fit statistics on validation labels or mix held-out examples into training.
