# ResNet

## Historical question and bottleneck

[Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385) (He et al., 2015) is the primary reference. The original question motivating this module is how to learn a residual correction around an identity shortcut rather than an unconstrained replacement. The practical bottleneck for this exercise is explicit: Tiny two-block teaching network; zero branch recovers identity followed by ReLU. This is a reduced teaching adaptation, not a claim that the paper used our dataset or compute budget.

## Mechanism and equations

`h_next=ReLU(h+F(h)); d(h+F(h))/dh=I+J_F.`

Derive each tensor's dimensions before implementing the equation. State the normalization axis, numerical stabilizer and train/evaluation behavior. Distinguish a mathematical mechanism from the full original training system.

## Implementation notes

Input/task: **mnist**. Output: **[N,1,28,28] -> [N,10] logits**. Optimize or report **accuracy** in the **maximize** direction. Tiny two-block teaching network; zero branch recovers identity followed by ReLU.

An executable bounded reference and mechanism tests are available; work in the starter before opening the editorial.

## Controlled experiment

Remove identity shortcuts while preserving both branch convolutions. Keep split, seed, optimizer schedule and training budget fixed unless that factor is the experimental variable. Report raw quality, wall time and parameter count, not an unsupported superiority claim.

## Pitfalls and checks

- Tiny two-block teaching network; zero branch recovers identity followed by ReLU.
- Never fit statistics on validation labels or mix held-out examples into training.
- Check shape and finite outputs before interpreting quality; an unrelated baseline cannot satisfy the intended mechanism.
- Draft targets and safety limits have not been GPU-calibrated. No pass threshold is advertised.
