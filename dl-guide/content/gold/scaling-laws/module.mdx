# Neural scaling laws

## Historical question and bottleneck

[Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361) (Kaplan et al., 2020) is the primary reference. The original question motivating this module is how to fit held-out loss versus parameters data and compute; aim for loss prediction within five percent on unseen runs. The practical bottleneck for this exercise is explicit: Fit only training runs; hold out larger compute scales. This is a reduced teaching adaptation, not a claim that the paper used our dataset or compute budget.

## Mechanism and equations

`L(N,D)=L_infinity+A/N^alpha+B/D^beta; held-out relative error=abs(pred-actual)/actual.`

Derive each tensor's dimensions before implementing the equation. State the normalization axis, numerical stabilizer and train/evaluation behavior. Distinguish a mathematical mechanism from the full original training system.

## Implementation notes

Input/task: **measured-training-runs**. Output: **Run descriptors -> predicted held-out loss**. Optimize or report **relative_loss_prediction_error** in the **minimize** direction. Fit only training runs; hold out larger compute scales.

This is a draft skeleton: reference training and task-specific tests are not implemented. The explicit stub must not be mistaken for a finished model.

## Controlled experiment

Fit without the irreducible-loss term and compare extrapolation error. Keep split, seed, optimizer schedule and training budget fixed unless that factor is the experimental variable. Report raw quality, wall time and parameter count, not an unsupported superiority claim.

## Pitfalls and checks

- Fit only training runs; hold out larger compute scales.
- Never fit statistics on validation labels or mix held-out examples into training.
- Check shape and finite outputs before interpreting quality; an unrelated baseline cannot satisfy the intended mechanism.
- Draft targets and safety limits have not been GPU-calibrated. No pass threshold is advertised.
