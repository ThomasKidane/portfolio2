# U-Net

## Historical question and bottleneck

[U-Net: Convolutional Networks for Biomedical Image Segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597) (Ronneberger et al., 2015) is the primary reference. The original question motivating this module is how to segment generated geometric shapes with aligned encoder-decoder skip features and report intersection over union. The practical bottleneck for this exercise is explicit: Nearest-neighbor target resize; exact spatial skip alignment. This is a reduced teaching adaptation, not a claim that the paper used our dataset or compute budget.

## Mechanism and equations

`decoder_level=conv(concat(upsample(decoder_next),encoder_skip)); IoU=intersection/union.`

Derive each tensor's dimensions before implementing the equation. State the normalization axis, numerical stabilizer and train/evaluation behavior. Distinguish a mathematical mechanism from the full original training system.

## Implementation notes

Input/task: **generated-shapes**. Output: **[N,C,H,W] -> [N,K,H,W] mask logits**. Optimize or report **iou** in the **maximize** direction. Nearest-neighbor target resize; exact spatial skip alignment.

This is a draft skeleton: reference training and task-specific tests are not implemented. The explicit stub must not be mistaken for a finished model.

## Controlled experiment

Remove skip concatenations at matched decoder capacity. Keep split, seed, optimizer schedule and training budget fixed unless that factor is the experimental variable. Report raw quality, wall time and parameter count, not an unsupported superiority claim.

## Pitfalls and checks

- Nearest-neighbor target resize; exact spatial skip alignment.
- Never fit statistics on validation labels or mix held-out examples into training.
- Check shape and finite outputs before interpreting quality; an unrelated baseline cannot satisfy the intended mechanism.
- Draft targets and safety limits have not been GPU-calibrated. No pass threshold is advertised.
